01 — About

A microbiologist who learned to
speak in models.

I started in the wet lab — culturing microbes, running PCRs, learning how messy real biology is. That grounding is why I care about interpretability: a model that predicts cancer subtype is only useful if a biologist can see why.

Now, as an M.Sc. Bioinformatics student at Garden City University, Bengaluru, my work centers on integrating multi-omics data into machine-learning pipelines that are reproducible, explainable, and tied back to biology. I work end-to-end: from raw TCGA acquisition and normalization through modeling, survival analysis, and explainable-AI attribution.

My goal is to become a computational biologist contributing to precision oncology — building AI systems that don't just classify, but explain.

02 — Research

Three problems I'm working on.

Each project pairs a hard biological question with a computational system built to answer it interpretably.

PROJECT 01 · ONGOING

Explainable AI for Multi-Omics Integration in Pancreatic Cancer

Python · PyTorch · XGBoost · SHAP · Streamlit · TCGA-PAAD
  • End-to-end multi-omics ML platform on real TCGA-PAAD data (160 patients) for PDAC molecular subtype classification (classical vs. basal-like) and survival prediction.
  • Integrates transcriptomic, genomic, methylation, mutation, proteomic and radiomic layers through a unified pipeline with TMM/VST normalization and ComBat-Seq batch correction.
  • Explainable-AI layer (SHAP, SurvSHAP(t), LRP) surfaces biologically interpretable biomarkers driving each prediction.
  • Delivered as an interactive Streamlit dashboard spanning QC, AutoML, XAI, survival, and pathway exploration.

PROJECT 02 · ONGOING

In Silico Multi-Epitope Vaccine Design Against Hantavirus

Immunoinformatics · Reverse Vaccinology · Epitope Prediction · Structural Modeling
  • Designing a multi-epitope subunit vaccine construct through a reverse-vaccinology and immunoinformatics workflow.
  • Predicting and screening B-cell and T-cell (MHC-I/II) epitopes for antigenicity, immunogenicity, allergenicity, and toxicity.
  • Assembling epitopes with suitable linkers and adjuvants, followed by structure prediction, refinement, and in silico immune-response simulation.
PROJECT 03 · ONGOING

Structural & Functional Analysis of the CCR5 Receptor

Structural Bioinformatics · Homology Modeling · Sequence Analysis
  • Investigating structure–function relationships of CCR5, a key co-receptor in HIV-1 entry.
  • Performing sequence and structural analysis, conserved-domain identification, and visualization of functionally critical residues.
  • Linking structural features to therapeutic and antiviral relevance in a compiled research report.
